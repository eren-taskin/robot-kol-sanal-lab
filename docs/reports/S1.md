# S1 — Super-twisting sliding mode controller (STSMC) and comparison

## What was built

- `SuperTwistingSlidingMode` (Core/Control, sealed, `IController` only, no allocation in `Compute`, its own `ControllerBelief`). The law is s = ė + Λe, u = M̂(q̄)(q̈_d + Λė + v) + ĥ + τ̂f, v = k1|s|^½ sign(s) + w and ẇ = k2 sign(s), integrated once per sample. It uses CT's mid-hold prediction (it applies, on by default), CT+I-style anti-windup, and an optional boundary layer ε (default 0).
- `ControlMethod.STSMC`, plus `ControllerSettings.Lambda`, `K1`, `K2` and `BoundaryLayer` in the scenario and its JSON. Library scenario "Yanlış model – STSMC, 0,3 kg habersiz yük".
- Web: "Süper-twisting kayan kipli (STSMC)" in the controller lists. The gain table shows Λ, k1, k2, and there is an ε field. The stability panel and the analysis page show "Bu kontrolcü doğrusallaştırılamaz; kök yer eğrisi ve Bode uygulanamaz". The analysis panels and the tuner are off for it. The design helper is hidden for it. `GainTuner` refuses STSMC.

## Gains

L was measured on the library path for plant 0.3 kg / belief 0 kg: **[0, 93.79, 198.96, 1013.00] rad/s³**. Joint 1 takes joint 2's value. With k1 = 1.5√L and k2 = 1.1L this gives k1 = [14.53, 14.53, 21.16, 47.74] and k2 = [103.2, 103.2, 218.9, 1114.3]. Λ = 20 1/s. Details are in docs/DECISIONS.md D13.

**Summary.**

- **Underestimate:** the expected result did not hold. With the standard gains STSMC is worse than CT+I: its wrist RMS is 3.3 × CT+I's, and the wrist drifts into its joint limit at 0.25 s. The wrist needs about 1500 rad/s² of correction already at t = 0, and w4 builds up at only 1114 rad/s³.
- **Overestimate:** STSMC is the clear winner. It never clamps (peak |u| 1.23 N·m), and its RMS error is 2 % of CT+I's. CT and CT+I chatter between the torque limits.
- **Boundary layer:** ε = 0.5 rad/s cuts the overestimate chattering by about 370×.
- **Gain scale:** no single scale of L makes both cases work (`docs/results/stsmc_gain_scale.csv`).
- **Sampling:** on a 1-DOF double integrator the sliding band scales as Ts². Going from Ts = 2 ms to 1 ms divides it by 4.016.
